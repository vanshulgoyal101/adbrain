# Click-to-WhatsApp research: Indian home services

Issue #1132 · Analyst (headless) · 2026-10-28

## Scope and method

This is a public-information scan for W1 (tap-to-send questions) and W2 (a concrete reason to send). It uses Meta's click-to-message guidance, WhatsApp Business guidance, and repeatable public Meta Ad Library searches for India. The Ad Library is dynamic and its UI does not expose a stable text export, so the examples below describe the pattern rather than copying an advertiser's wording. No phone numbers, page IDs, or other business contact data are included.

Sources:

- [Meta: ads that click to message](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/click-to-message-ads) — make the messaging action explicit; use “Send message”/“Chat now”-style calls to action; explain the value and what to expect; use a greeting that prompts a reply.
- [WhatsApp Business app](https://whatsappbusiness.com/products/business-app/) — greet people when they start a conversation, respond when away, and make the business profile helpful at first contact.

## Ten recurring patterns

| # | Pattern and public example (described) | W1/W2 implication |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The action is unmistakable.** Home-service ads put WhatsApp/chat language in the visual or primary text, not only in the button. This matches Meta's click-to-message guidance. | Greeting should say what to send next; avoid a bare “Hello”. |
| 2 | **A specific first outcome beats “know more”.** Solar and home-improvement ads commonly lead with a quote, estimate, inspection, or eligibility check. | Offer one concrete deliverable in the greeting and prefill. |
| 3 | **Low-friction inspection/visit.** Service ads use a survey, inspection, measurement, or site visit as the first step before a sale. | Ask a booking question, rather than asking the customer to explain the entire job. |
| 4 | **Price/eligibility is a natural opener.** AC, pest-control, interiors, and solar searches surface price, subsidy, or package questions as the practical first concern. | Include one price/eligibility question, but do not promise an unverified number. |
| 5 | **Local context reduces uncertainty.** Indian service ads foreground city/area coverage and sometimes ask for the locality before scheduling. | Ask for city/area only when the owner can serve it; keep the question short. |
| 6 | **Problem-first wording.** Cooling, leakage, high bill, pests, and renovation needs are framed as the customer's problem before the provider's credentials. | Phrase questions in the customer's voice (“Can you check…?”), not as a product label. |
| 7 | **Trust proof accompanies the ask.** Service creatives pair the action with experience, warranty, trained technicians, or transparent pricing. | Put proof in the ad; keep the WhatsApp question focused on one next step. |
| 8 | **Time expectation is part of the offer.** Click-to-message guidance says to clarify what people should expect; service ads often signal same-day/fast response. | Use a response-time line only after the owner commits to it and can staff it. |
| 9 | **Language follows the audience.** India-facing home-service promotions mix English with Hindi or Roman Hindi; the useful distinction is script and customer comprehension, not a literal translation. | Test English, Hindi, or Hinglish as separate runs; do not mix scripts in one question. |
| 10 | **One tap, one intent.** The strongest message setups offer a small set of distinct questions (price, booking, eligibility/status), rather than three variants of “send details”. | W1 should offer three mutually exclusive intents, each ≤40 characters. |

These are directional patterns, not claims that any one advertiser's copy is the causal reason for performance. The measurable test remains open-to-message rate, followed by reply time and cost per enquiry.

## Solaride W1 candidates

All strings are 40 characters or fewer, including spaces and punctuation. They use only the currently documented Solaride offers: free site survey, subsidy help, and lower electricity bills.

### Set A — English

1. `How much subsidy can I get?` (27)
2. `Can I book a free site survey?` (30)
3. `How much will my bill go down?` (30)

### Set B — Hinglish (Roman script)

1. `Mujhe kitni subsidy milegi?` (27)
2. `Free site survey book karni hai` (31)
3. `Mera bijli bill kitna kam hoga?` (31)

### Set C — Hindi (Devanagari)

1. `मुझे कितनी सब्सिडी मिलेगी?` (26)
2. `मुफ़्त साइट सर्वे बुक करना है` (29)
3. `मेरा बिजली बिल कितना घटेगा?` (27)

Use one language set per run, matching the ad language. The owner should approve the set and keep the existing one-lever-per-run design; W1 replaces the prefill and call button with ice breakers.

## Solaride W2 reason-to-send lines

These are candidate lines, not approved claims. They should be added as Brand offers and used only if the owner can fulfil them consistently:

1. `Get a solar subsidy estimate on WhatsApp`
2. `Send your bill for a quick solar estimate`
3. `Check your home's solar savings on WhatsApp`

The first is the safest starting hypothesis because it is concrete without inventing a savings amount. A time promise (for example, “within 2 minutes”) must be approved separately and staffed for the whole run. The greeting, prefill, and ad should promise the same deliverable; do not silently substitute a generic information request when the owner cannot fulfil it.

## Recommendation for CPO/CEO

Approve Set A for the next W1 run if English remains the creative language; reserve Set B for a later language run. For W2, test candidate 1 only after the owner confirms the response process and response-time SLA. Compare open-to-message rate at similar spend, then median first-reply time and cost per real enquiry. One run should change only W1 or W2, not both.
